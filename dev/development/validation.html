Validation#

How closely PYSTILT’s results match a reference, and how fast its slow steps are, as measured. The user guides give the setting to use; this page gives the numbers behind it, each with the test or study that measured it, so you can judge how far to trust a result and rerun the check yourself.

Footprints against STILT-R#

Every footprint cell matches STILT-R to a relative tolerance of 1e-7, for the same particles, on a pinned STILT-R commit. The fidelity tests run both on the same HYSPLIT output in CI. What is compared, and the few settings that do not match on purpose, are in STILT-R Parity.

Met stops that pass as domain exits#

A run whose met runs out partway fails as MET_COVERAGE when a particle at the last step is more than 5 grid cells from every side of the met’s grid (Checking A Run). Particles that leave the domain end at most 2.0 cells inside it, on HRRR (3 km), NAM (12 km) and NARR (32 km) grids and their crops.

On domains a hundred cells across or more, every met stop tested was caught: the particle furthest inside was 34 cells in or more. On crops 45 to 120 cells across, 3 to 9 % of the met stops tested passed as domain exits. None of them was in the first 6 hours of a run, at most one particle in six was still in the air, and under 3 % of the footprint was lost. So on a small crop, a run cut short late by the met can look complete. The rule and its reasons are in Architecture (“Implementation notes”).

Met sampled at particle positions#

stilt.sampling.sample_met() reads mlht, dens, pres and zsfc from the met files at each particle’s position. On two 6-hour HRRR runs from Salt Lake City (tests/integration/test_sample_met.py), against HYSPLIT’s own columns:

Column

Difference from HYSPLIT

zsfc

A few meters.

mlht

A median of 16 to 30 m against a HYSPLIT run with kmixd: 0 (floored at kmix0). With the default kmixd: 3 HYSPLIT finds its own mixed layer from the profile, so the two are not comparable.

pres

A median of 0.02 hPa, 0.23 to 0.30 hPa at the 95th percentile, and 1.4 to 4.6 hPa at worst.

dens

0.17 to 0.57 % higher.

On one of those runs with a 10 km top, z_top=10000 gives a median pres difference of 0.024 hPa against 0.032 for the default 25 km, and about 0.29 hPa at the 95th percentile with either. Terrain-following files (flag 3) are read on their own levels rather than HYSPLIT’s internal ones, so their values differ more, by an amount not measured.

Pressure weighting#

The pressure-weighting transform (Particle Weighting (Transforms)) weights each particle of a column by the air mass of its release layer, from a fit of ln p against height. On a winter column at WBB in Salt Lake City, the least isothermal case in the test data, the fit explains more than 99 % of the variance in pressure. Its scale height is 6.5 to 9 km, and its surface pressure is 800 to 900 hPa, as the site’s 1,480 m elevation needs. Between runs of 100 and 1,000 particles, the weights’ total agrees to 2 % and the weighted footprint’s ratio to the unweighted one to 5 % (tests/integration/test_pressure_weighting.py).

Reading a large met archive#

The first time PYSTILT reads a folder of met files it reads each file’s header into a table. With 16 threads, 16,600 HRRR files on a network filesystem took about two minutes; one thread took 35. Later runs read the table.
